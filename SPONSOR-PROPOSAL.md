Draft for discussion with the fiscal sponsor. See also the hosted version:

## Where This Stands

A live donation intake page (`donate.html`, deployed at carnivalsociety.org/donate.html)
already explains the program to donors, collects vehicle details, and discloses up front
which of the two paths below applies to their donation.

## The Ask, In Two Parts

**Part One — Receive & Sell (low lift).** Standard nonprofit vehicle donation. The sponsor
is the receiving org; most donated vehicles route through a donation processor (CARS) and
convert to cash that funds CSI's classes, tools, art builds, and events. Donor receives a
sale-price receipt.

**Part Two — Retain for Art & Build Use (new).** Some vehicles are exactly what CSI needs
as material for builds and installations. **Proposed structure: the sponsor holds title on
retained vehicles; CSI has use of them.** Static use only, right now — art and build
material. Not operating, not carrying passengers, not on public roads. That boundary is
deliberate — it's what keeps the insurance question simple.

## The Retention Policy

A boundary the sponsor approves once — not a request to approve every vehicle individually.

- **Discretion**: CSI decides which donated vehicles to retain. No value cap, no type
  restriction.
- **Visibility**: the sponsor gets a running log (vehicle, donor, date retained, intended
  use) — viewable anytime, not approval per vehicle. Live at
  `members.carnivalsociety.org/vehicle-log.html` (sponsor creates their own login).
- **Scale check**: not a hard cap — if retained vehicles grow past a handful at once, CSI
  flags it proactively.
- **Paperwork**: photo, VIN, donor contact, and the FMV acknowledgment — issued once
  genuine, ongoing use is actually underway (see tax note below).
- **Pickup/transport**: CSI arranges this directly through community volunteers, with AAA
  as a fallback if needed — not routed through CARS, since CARS's free pickup is bundled
  with them taking the vehicle for sale.
- **Sale after use**: if a retained vehicle is later sold, net proceeds split between CSI
  and the sponsor. **Split ratio: TBD — decide with the sponsor.**

## Named, Not Asked

Future operational use (moving people and gear, possibly rides at events) is a separate
conversation about insurance, and possibly Colorado's Amusement Ride Safety Act. Not on the
table now — flagging it early just means it won't be a surprise later.

## Membership Perk

Every vehicle donation earns **six months of unlimited classes** — sewing, sign painting,
structures, fire, all of it. Live on the donation page.

## Reference — Tax Mechanics

| Deduction claimed | What's required |
|---|---|
| Under $500 | Minimal substantiation for the donor |
| $500–$5,000 | IRS Form 8283 and a written acknowledgment (Form 1098-C) |
| Over $5,000 | Independent appraisal required — unless "significant intervening use" applies (our retained-vehicle path) |

"Significant intervening use" requires genuine, ongoing use over time — not simply
possession. A retained vehicle parked without active use may not qualify, which is why the
FMV acknowledgment is issued only once real use is underway.
